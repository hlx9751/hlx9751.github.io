**HYRULE (海拉鲁)** uses a pair of Nintendo Joy-Con controllers to send Bluetooth signals, which are parsed in Max/MSP via OSC to manipulate audio and visual parameters. The title refers to Hyrule Continent, the central world of *The Legend of Zelda*, and the work is structured into three sections: "Birth," "Temples," and "Calamity Ganon."

The non-visual version of the work was performed at ICMC 2023. Later, visuals were integrated through TouchDesigner, emphasizing the interaction between sound and image. Unlike timeline-based playback in conventional music performance, this piece adopts an event-driven structure in which gestural input directly shapes musical progression.

This design strengthens performer feedback, supports improvisation, and increases the sense of play in performance. A chunk-based score further invites the audience to experience the work as if they were engaging a game world, turning musical control into a form of embodied navigation.

**Materials:** 8 speakers, Joy-Con controllers  
**Technique:** Spatial audio, custom gesture and motion-tracking software, audio analysis  
**Keywords:** Max/MSP, OSC, Logic Pro, TouchDesigner, interactive music
